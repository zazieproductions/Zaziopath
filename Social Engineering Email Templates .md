# **The Psychopathic Charmer**

### **"Nothing Personal"**

**Core motive:** Maximum influence with minimum attachment.

> Hi—

> Your film caught my attention.

> If you're still looking for music, I can probably help.

> Here's a reel.

> If it's useful, let's schedule twenty minutes.

> If not, no worries.

# **The Vulnerable Narcissist**

### **"The Only Composer Who Truly Understands Your Film"**

**Core motive:** Be seen as uniquely insightful.

> Hi Jack,

> I watched your trailer three times. Most people will probably tell you it needs bigger music. I actually think it needs less.

> What struck me wasn't the horror. It was the loneliness underneath it.

> That's the film I became interested in scoring.

> I've spent years writing music that lives in that space—where tension comes from emotional ambiguity instead of volume.

> If you've already had composers send you "cinematic horror," ignore this email.

> But if you're looking for someone who sees the quieter film hiding underneath the obvious one, I'd love to talk.

# **The Anti-Commercial Ally**

"I'm not like everyone chasing your attention."

> I almost didn't write because I'm sure your inbox is full of "I'd love to collaborate\!" emails.

> Truthfully, I don't enjoy networking very much.

> I only reach out when I find work that feels unusually sincere.

> Your film reminded me of why I started composing in the first place...

# **The Artist-Manipulator**

Makes the filmmaker feel chosen.

---

Alex,

This may sound strange.

I don't think I chose your film.

I think your film selected me.

I know that sounds ridiculous.

But every few years I encounter a project that appears to occupy exactly the psychological territory I've already been exploring privately.

This feels like one.

# **The Ghost of the Future**

*"I'm writing from your future career."*

> I suspect in five years this email will either look ridiculous or strangely inevitable.

> That's usually how interesting collaborations begin.

It creates narrative momentum.

## **5\. The Moral Minimalist**

They make their own request look so modest that refusing it appears disproportionate.

> I am not asking you to commit, change direction, or even schedule a call. Send me sixty seconds without temp music. I will return one idea. If it is useless, nothing has been lost.

The actual request may indeed be small, but the rhetorical design removes every respectable basis for refusal. Anyone who still says no can feel rigid, frightened, or irrationally defensive.
