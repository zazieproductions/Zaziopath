## ⬛ §0 · THE QUESTION, AND WHY IT NEEDS A CONTROL

Every reading in this vault — the six verdicts, the two case studies, the anthropologist, the visitor,
this report — reconstructs the same subject from the same corpus and arrives
at recognisably the same person: a young multidisciplinary artist in Asheville with self-reported
executive dysfunction, a large catalog, an evidentiary habit, an enormous psychological vocabulary,
a mythological practice, a commission habit aimed at machines, an unresolved relationship to
ordinary Tuesday conduct, and a documented tendency to answer questions about conduct with
documents about conduct.

Thirty thousand words of commentary have produced no serious disagreement about **who is described**.
They have produced elaborate disagreement about **what it means**. That is a suspicious pattern, and
it has a standard diagnostic: *a reconstruction that survives every reframing may not have been
derived from the evidence at all. It may have been derived from the vocabulary.*

The usual way to test that is an ablation. Delete one input, re-run the reconstruction, and see what
breaks. If the reading does not change when a category is removed, the category was not carrying the
reading. If the reading collapses when a category is removed, that category is the load-bearing wall,
whatever its size on the floor plan.

The vault has a floor plan. It has a colour code, a strata census, a byte-weight for every stratum
(FIG 6), and a finding that says **diagnosis outweighs treatment roughly 750 : 1.** That census is a
*cost* measurement. This report is a *dependency* measurement. They do not agree, and the disagreement
is the finding.

### The eight ablations

Run in the order the commission specified. Each removes one conceptual category entirely — files,
quotations, and derived inferences — and reconstructs the subject from the remainder.

| # | Ablation | Removed category |
|:--:|---|---|
| **A1** | **WITHOUT PSYCHOLOGY** | the interior — typologies, tests, shadow work, the compendium, parts-work, self-diagnosis |
| **A2** | **WITHOUT CAREER INFORMATION** | the economy — rates, clients, platforms, positioning, outreach, pricing, income |
| **A3** | **WITHOUT ARTISTIC OUTPUT** | the works — catalogues, manuscripts, compositions, constructed languages, the built objects |

## ⬛ §1 · METHOD

### 1.1 What an ablation is here, and what it is not

An ablation is not censorship and not a thought experiment about what the person *would* be like.
The person is not being ablated; the *paper trail* is. The question is always the same and always
mechanical:

> Given the remaining documents, **can the archive's central claims still be reconstructed — and by
> what route?**

Two failure modes are named in advance, because both are easy and both are useless:

- **The sentimental error** — treating the removed category as a soul, mourning it, and declaring
  the subject "destroyed." Nothing here destroys anyone. A file listing is not a life.
- **The forensic error** — treating the reconstruction as a discovery. Every reconstruction below is
  *already present* in the corpus, usually in the README or a later audit. Ablation does not produce
  new knowledge about the subject. It produces knowledge about the corpus's load distribution.

### 1.2 The control

The control is the un-ablated reading, which is not restated here because the vault has nine of them
already. Its one-sentence form, for comparison:

> **Control:** a self-directed multidisciplinary artist with a large catalog and an unusually
> articulate self-analysis habit, who converted an evidentiary anxiety into a working archive,
> commissioned adversarial machine readings of himself, kept the unflattering findings, declared
> behaviour the endpoint, and left that endpoint deliberately unrecorded.

Every ablation below is scored on how much of that sentence survives and by what route.

### 1.3 The four readers

"The archive" does not read itself. It is read by at least four incompatible readers, and they have
**different dependency profiles** — a fact which this experiment establishes and which no prior
document in the vault has measured. All scoring below is per-reader.

| | Reader | Corpus | Scale | Built |
|:--:|---|---|---|---|
| **⟨S⟩** | **The self-account** | `README.md` + the memory export's self-description | ~10,000 words | by the maker |
| **⟨T⟩** | **The tribunal** | `verdict_from_A.i`–`_6` + both case studies | ~30,000 (164,175 B) | by machines, commissioned by the maker |
| **⟨A⟩** | **The audit layer** | case file · provenance ledger · governance handbook · deep gap audit · rabbit holes · meta-analysis · visitor's notebook · anthropological field report | ~38,000 (212,247 B, 8 documents) | against the maker, by the maker and machines together |
| **⟨B⟩** | **The outside biographer** | hostile dossier + negative-space biography | ~7,000 words | at the maker's request, against the maker |

**Scoring notation**

`●` **intact** — the reader's claims survive with their support intact
`◐` **degraded** — the reader survives; its central or distinguishing claims lose support
`○` **collapse** — the reader's central claims lose their support entirely
`=` **invariant** — the reader is **byte-identical**; the ablation is undetectable from inside it

The notation matters more than it looks. `◐` means the reader loses something. `=` means the reader
was never attached to the ablated thing in the first place. Two of the eight experiments below
produce `=` for a reader, and those two cells are the most informative results in the report.

### 1.4 Recovery definition

A claim is **recovered** if, after ablation, it can still be reached from a primary file by a stated
route. It is **quoted** if it survives only as prose in a document that no longer has its source.
Quotation is not recovery. This distinction is the vault's own (`DEEP_GAP_AUDIT.md` §3.4) and it does
most of the work in §5.

### 1.5 The classification problem, stated before the results

**The eight categories are not orthogonal, and the corpus says so itself.** Eighteen root files
(1,926.9 KB — 17% of total categorised mass) belong to two ablations at once: the residency dossier is
artistic output *and* first-person voice; the CV is career *and* voice; the memory export is an AI
conversation *and* voice; the catalog is artistic output *and* achievement evidence; four mythographic
files are also authored works.

This is not a flaw in the experiment. **It is Experiment Zero, and it is the first result:**

> **Result R-A0 — the strata do not separate.** When the vault's own colour code is used as a
> classification, 18 of 98 files and 17% of mass refuse a single label. The claim that the vault is
> organised into eight distinct strata is true of its *diagrams* and false of its *files*. Where two
> categories claim the same file, an ablation of either one leaves the other's access route open —
> which is why so many of the results below show claims surviving the deletion of their apparent
> source. **Non-separability is the mechanism of provenance laundering, and it is structural, not
> psychological.**

Eighteen further root files (463.4 KB) fit no category in the commissioned set at all. Fourteen of
them are the reflexive layer — visitor's notebook, anthropologist, hostile biographer, rabbit holes,
negative-space biography, the four index files, `meta-experiment`, `create a link.md`, `text.txt`, and
one case-report PDF. Two are format twins: the hostile dossier and the visitor's notebook each exist
twice, once as Markdown and once as PDF, which means the same content is counted twice in the root mass
and neither version carries a genre or provenance note distinguishing it from the other. Two are
generator scripts. That ninth stratum was not commissioned, so this report runs an uncommissioned
ninth ablation on it (§7) because it produced the single largest result in the experiment.

everything. It is therefore the only category in this experiment whose removal cannot be performed by
deleting files — which is precisely why it took a separate method (§4.6) and produced the largest
result in the report.

## ⬛ §3 · HOW TO READ THE EIGHT RECONSTRUCTIONS

Each ablation below is reported in the same six parts, and the order is deliberate.

1. **Removed** — the exact file set and its measured mass. Nothing is inferred; the files are named.
2. **The control** — what the un-ablated reading says about the same subject, so the deletion has
   something to be compared against.
3. **Reconstruction, per reader** — `⟨S⟩`, `⟨T⟩`, `⟨A⟩`, `⟨B⟩` in turn, scored on the §1.3 scale.
4. **What survives completely unchanged** — the most easily skipped part and the most informative.
5. **The Zazie who disappears** — one sentence, in the commissioned register.
6. **Tier and falsifier** — the vault's own discipline, applied to its own experiment.

A note on tone. These reconstructions are written in the register of the material they describe,
because a reconstruction of this subject in a flat voice would be a misdescription. The register is
not evidence and should not be read as an argument. Where a sentence is doing interpretive work rather
than reporting a measurement, it is marked `strong_inference` and given a falsifier.

silence), the whole audit summer (a response to the quietest year on record), and the commission

The README minus psychology keeps: the colour code, two Mermaid maps, the vault inventory, the

What remains is *architecture without a building*: a beautifully specified instrument with no

### Reconstruction ⟨T⟩ — ○ collapse

Five of six verdicts are psychological readings. With psychology removed they become process

The corpus's central claim — *insight without behaviour* — survives, but only as a claim about
documents. Which is, notably, the only form in which the meta-analysis already said it could survive.

### Reconstruction ⟨A⟩ — ◐ degraded — **and this is the first surprise**

The audit layer is the most psychology-resistant reader in the building. Nine of the case file's ten
findings (F-01, F-02, F-03, F-04, F-05, F-06, F-07, F-09, F-10) are derived from the catalog CSV and

One finding dies completely: **F-08, "the specimen cabinet fits one lock,"** which is *entirely* a
cross-read of the specimen files against the memory export's hidden-pattern register. With the
psychology gone, the cabinet is exactly what it says it is on the tin — a general taxonomy of
manipulation.

> **Result R-A1 — the audit layer is a conversion of the psychological corpus, not an extension of it.**
> The vault's evidence discipline did not grow out of its psychology; it grew out of noticing that
> the psychological layer was where the unfalsifiable claims lived. Remove psychology and the audit
> layer barely notices. This is the strongest available internal evidence that the 2026-09-23
> governance turn was a genuine change of method rather than another register of the same practice.

### Reconstruction ⟨B⟩ — ◐ degraded

The outside biographer keeps its first, third and fifth rows (who is the maker; what kind of maker;
relationship to truth and invention) and loses its fourth and sixth (why build Zaziopath; what can
be said about the person beyond the work). Its "claims withdrawn" list — eleven items — shrinks by
half, because half of them are withdrawals of psychological claims that no longer exist to withdraw.

### What survives **completely unchanged** — and this is the deflation

The *outer* description of the subject does not require psychology at all. From the surviving files
alone, a stranger reconstructs: a North Carolina artist and LLC; a catalog of 182 release
appearances across 21 releases and 2,444 days; a self-authored CV; a 133-record public-web census; a
body of constructed-language and mythographic writing; a browser console whose only function is to
record one dated line of behaviour locally; and **a documented habit of commissioning adversarial
machine readings and filing all of them, including the ones that fail.**

That last item is the whole archive's most distinctive behaviour, and it is legible *without a single
psychological document*. The subject's most unusual fact is public conduct, not interior structure.

### The Zazie who disappears — **the interior one, and only the interior one**

> **A1 verdict:** psychology is **49% of the mass and none of the outer description.** Removing it
> removes the *explanation* of the archive and leaves the *archive*. What disappears is the causal
> engine; what remains is a person with an unusual filing habit and no stated reason for it.
>
> *Dependence: 2 readers collapse, 2 degrade. Mass: 5,461.6 KB. Dependency per kilobyte: the lowest
> of the four "load-bearing" categories. Psychology is the vault's **volume**, not its **spine**.*

**Tier:** `strong_inference` — the file sets are measured, the reader collapses are reasoned from
stating which claims cite which files.
**Falsifier:** a claim in the surviving corpus — CV, catalog, media census, fiction, audit layer —
whose support runs through a psychological document. I found none; the case file's glosses are
decorative to its findings, and the CV does not cite psychology anywhere.

## ⬛ §4.4 · A6 — WITHOUT CHRONOLOGY

*Ordered out of sequence because it is the largest result and belongs next to the other three.*

**Removed.** Every date, timestamp, duration, sequence, lag and interval — in filenames, headers,
tables, prose and embedded metadata. Nothing is deleted from disk, because chronology has no files.
The words remain; the *when* does not.

### The measurement that makes this the decisive ablation

| | |
|---|---|
| Catalog span (earliest → latest release) | **2,444 days** — 2019-09-09 → 2026-05-19 |
| Entire interpretive + governance + judicial apparatus | **26 days** — 2026-09-02 → 2026-09-28 |
| Repo creation → last document | 32 days |
| Visible commits in this checkout | **1** |

The archive presents itself as a longitudinal record: eight years of practice, a 2022 registration
sweep, a quiet year, an audit summer, three weeks between the sixth verdict and the case study. Its
*apparatus* — the whole of ⟨T⟩, ⟨A⟩ and the judicial fiction — was composed inside twenty-six days.

That would be fine if the version history corroborated the dates. **It cannot.** The checkout carries
one grafted commit; DEEP_GAP_AUDIT §2.1 records it and the correct reading (`import, squash merge, or
graft — not psychological`). The consequence is epistemic, not psychological: **the repository cannot
independently corroborate its own chronology.** Every date in it is a self-report. Every timestamped
finding inherits the self-report's provenance. The house rule — *an undated insight is a mood* —
protects the vault from undated claims and does nothing at all about *self-dated* ones.

The README's §11 provenance table, §07a's timeline, the curation-pass record, the review intervals,

is still a fine constitution. It is no longer an instrument, because the instrument's only moving part
is a calendar.

V1 is undated — and the corpus treats that as a defect (PX-3b: five of six self-date to 2026-09-02).
The most-quoted finding in the corpus, *"zero behaviour changes,"* is a chronological claim about a

The meta-analysis's headline structural finding — *no document after V1 opened a new primary source* —
is a claim about sequence. Remove chronology and the corpus's two best observations become unsayable.

## ⬛ §6 · THREE TESTS THAT SHARPEN THE RESULT

### 6.1 The copy test — does the archive know where its claims came from?

For each of five central claims: how many mutually independent strata hold a copy, and how deep is the
chain to the earliest located source? Copies in *derived* documents do not count as support; they count
as circulation.

| Claim | Earliest located origin | Copies across strata | Independent re-derivations | Survives deletion of its origin? |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| *The subject discounts completed achievements* | one line in the assistant memory export | **6** | **0** | **yes** |
| *200 tracks · 165 verified ISRCs* | the workbook's STATISTICS sheet | **17** | **2 — and both dissent** | **yes** |
| *Six verdicts are one sequential series* | the `🔗 Connected` blocks in the verdicts themselves | 4 | 1 (legitimate — the blocks are primary) | yes |
| *The 2022 registration event* | **the CSV itself** — the year embedded in ISRC characters 6–7 | 4 | **1 — and it reproduces** | **no** |
| *No committed stewardship receipt was located* | a `git ls-files` search recorded in the deep gap audit | 6 | 1 — and it reproduces | **no** |

Two of five claims have a *recomputable* derivation. Both reproduce. Three of five have a **voice or a
summary** as origin. None of the three can be falsified by anyone, and all three survive the deletion
of their source, because the copy is everywhere and the origin is nowhere.

> **Result R-C1 — the archive's provenance depth is one and its copy depth is six.** A claim that
> originated in a single sentence in a memory export is now load-bearing in six strata, and no reader
> can find the sentence, because the sentence's file is not where the claim is argued. This is
> `DEEP_GAP_AUDIT` §3.2's "evidence laundering by circulation," which that audit predicted structurally.
> This report confirms it by ablation: **remove the origin and the claim does not weaken — it becomes
> better supported, because the only document that could contradict it is gone.**

### 6.2 The anchor test — what is the prose actually attached to?

Measured across the whole root corpus (all `.md`, `.html`, and the six verdicts):

| Test | Result |
|---|:--:|
| **ISRC codes quoted anywhere in the prose corpus** | **1** |
| …and it appears in `ChatGPT Personality Dump.md`, a memory file, not in an argument | — |
| Distinct track titles named by all six verdicts combined | **2** |
| Catalog **rows**, **cells**, or **sheets** cited by any verdict or case study | **0** |
| Documents naming at least one specific track title | 12 of 116 |

The vault's chosen emblem of un-fakeable proof is the ISRC — *"the ISRCs are affidavits sworn after the

Every interpretation in the corpus is attached to an **aggregate** (200, 182, 165, 142, 58, 133, 21,

> **Result R-C2 — the archive's unit of evidence is the summary, not the work.** This is worth stating
> exactly, because it is neither the accusation nor the compliment the vault has been given so far.
> It is not that the interpretations are unfounded. It is that **the level at which the archive reasons

### 6.3 The reconciliation the corpus never ran

`PX-2r` records that the workbook's headline numbers were never produced in evidence. Produced here, by
parsing the `.xlsx` directly.

**Workbook (`Zazie_Productions_Complete_Discography.xlsx`), CATALOG sheet:**

| | |
|---|--:|
| Numbered rows | **200** |
| …of which Album / EP / Single / Deluxe tracks | 179 |
| …of which features on other artists' releases | 5 |
| …of which compilation appearances and placeholders | **16** |
| Rows carrying an ISRC | **165** |
| Distinct ISRC codes among them | **145** |
| Rows with no ISRC (`—`) | 35 |

**Comma-separated export (`Zazie_Productions_Discography.csv`):**

| | |
|---|--:|
| Data rows | **182** |
| …credited to Zazie Productions | 178 |
| …credited to another artist | 4 |
| Rows carrying an ISRC | 161 |
| Distinct ISRC codes | **142** |
| Rows containing the literal string `N/A` | 21 |

**What the reconciliation shows.**

1. **"200 tracks" is not 200 of any one thing.** It is 179 own-release tracks + 5 features + 16
   compilation rows. One of those 16 reads, verbatim: `— No Zazie track found — Not in Discogs 58`.
   Another reads `50+ VA Appearances — See Discogs 11354435 / Linktree`. A row-count that includes
   placeholders has been reported as a track count in seventeen documents.
2. **"165 verified ISRCs" is a row count, not a recording count, and the workbook contains its own
   counter-example.** 165 rows carry a code; only 145 codes are distinct. The workbook therefore
   supports two different coverage figures for two different units — **82.5% of rows** (165/200) and
   **72.5% of rows counted as distinct recordings** (145/200) — and a third above 90% if the
   denominator is narrowed to the 179 own-release rows. The repository publishes one, and it is the one
   whose denominator includes 21 rows that are not its own tracks.
3. **The two internal sheets disagree with the CATALOG sheet about individual releases.** `The
   Triangular Savant` is listed at 1 track in LEGEND and STATISTICS (`0/1` ISRC verified) and has 6 rows
   in CATALOG. `G7e Torpedo` is listed at 6 tracks in LEGEND and STATISTICS and appears in the CSV
   zero times. Fifteen ISRC codes appear in one file and not the other — nine workbook-only, six
   CSV-only — and three more appear in both with different row counts: **eighteen codes whose
   row-count differs between the two files.**
4. **The README's scope explanation is therefore approximately right in kind and unresolvable in
   quantity.** C-01 of the ledger — *"200 versus 182,"* status *unresolved* — resolves to: the
   difference is real, and it is not one difference. It is at least four (six tracks absent from the
   CSV; five tracks present only in the CATALOG sheet; sixteen compilation rows; five feature rows),
   partially cancelling.

> **Result R-C3 — the archive's one designated verifiable layer does not reconcile with itself, and
> the discrepancy is not a documentation error but a unit error.** `N/A` is treated as a value in the
> CSV (21 rows); a placeholder row is counted as a track in the workbook; a feature on another artist's
> release is counted toward the subject's own total. The three are separate mistakes and they share one
> cause: **the repository has never fixed what a "track" is**, which is exactly the ontology gap
> `DEEP_GAP_AUDIT` §4 named, here confirmed with numbers from inside the file that was taken on faith.

## ⬛ §11 · WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT CANNOT SHOW

Filed first among its own limits, because a report about dependency that hides its dependencies is not
worth the paper.

1. **The categories were mine, not the archive's.** The commission named eight; I chose the file sets.
   A different cutter gets different masses and possibly different collapses. §1.5's non-separability
   result is a *consequence* of my cutting, and a different cut would produce a smaller A0.
2. **Ablation is not causation.** "The tribunal does not depend on the artwork" does not mean the
   artwork is unimportant, or that its absence would not matter to the maker, or that the works are
   not the point of the repository. The works are very likely the point. They are simply not the
   *support* of the interpretation that has grown around them.
3. **Not one PDF and not one DOCX in this repository was parsed by this report.** No PDF or DOCX
   reader is available in this environment, and the tree carries 42 tracked PDFs. Four of them have
   Markdown twins that were read instead (`HOSTILE_BIOGRAPHER_DOSSIER`, `VISITORS_NOTEBOOK`,
   `ZAZIOPATH — Collected Readings`); every other binary interior is described here from in-repository
   quotations of it, which is a weaker source by exactly the standard this report applies to others.
   **This is the largest limitation on the whole exercise**: a claim anchored inside `The Black Book
   II`, `Counterference Engine`, `This_Is_My_Design`, the compendium, or the six-sheet identity vault
   would be invisible to it. Where this report says a PDF's contents, it means a Markdown twin or a
   quotation, and it says so.
4. **The invariance measurements are corpus-negative, not corpus-exhaustive.** "No verdict cites a row"
   is a search result over a defined file set with a stated pattern (§6.2). It is falsifiable by one
   counter-example and I have tried to state it so that it invites one.
5. **The composition-window finding (2,444 : 26) is arithmetic, not psychology.** It says what the
   dates say. It does not say that anything was hidden, backfilled, or fabricated. It says that the
   apparatus has a composition window two orders of magnitude smaller than the period it describes,
   and that the version history cannot corroborate the difference.
6. **No reader was varied.** All eight reconstructions use this reader. A machine reader with different
   priors, or the maker reading this report, would produce different reconstructions from the same
   remainders. That is a property of readings, not a defect of the method, but it means the collapse
   scores in §5 are *one reader's* dependency profile and not a constant.
7. **No diagnosis is offered and none is possible from this material.** Every ablation above concerns
   documents. Seven of the eight categories could be deleted and the person would be exactly the same
   size.

## ⬛ §12 · THE NEXT ABLATION

The commission asked which information secretly carries the interpretation. This report answers:
**the date and the voice.** That answer is checkable, and it comes with a test that would settle it.

> **A10 — THE UNDATED VOICELESS READING.**
> Take the fourteen first-person documents and strip every date and every first-person verb. Do not
> delete the files. Remove the tense and the "I" and leave the nouns.
>
> If the archive survives — if a competent reader can still reconstruct the subject from the stripped
> corpus — then F-5 and F-6 are wrong, the load-bearing analysis is an artefact of my cutting, and the

> correct one.
>
> If the stripped corpus reconstructs a *genre* rather than a *person* — a method, a set of documents,

> and the most useful thing anyone could add to this repository is not another interpretation.
> It is a date that can be checked by someone else, attached to one sentence that somebody said.

The undated, voiceless version of this archive is roughly eight megabytes of excellent method and no
one in the room. That is not a criticism. It is a measurement, and like all of this report's
measurements, it is falsifiable by one document that does it differently.
