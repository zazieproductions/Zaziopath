# The Person Who Exists Between Versions

*A negative-space biography. Not a profile assembled from the records, but the seams they leave visible.*

## Name / instrument

The repository names **Zazie Kanwar-Torge** as its subject; it also names **Zazie Productions LLC** as the subject. Elsewhere, “Zazie Productions” is not merely a legal or production label but the auteur: a sound, a signature, an aesthetic, a public-facing person. The records move without warning among the individual, the company, the artist name, and the body of work. They do not give a rule for when a statement about one transfers to another. The brand is called a mask in one register, then used as the face in another. [README](README.md) · [HOSTILE_BIOGRAPHER_DOSSIER](HOSTILE_BIOGRAPHER_DOSSIER.md)

The identity card calls the subject an experimental musician and multimedia artist; the CVs divide that practice into different public frames, including an electroacoustic-specific version. The typological vault supplies still another kind of identity: a dense grid of types, traits, and scores. These may all be deliberate angles, not mutually exclusive identities. But the archive leaves open whether they are translations for different audiences, competing definitions, or descriptions of different objects sharing a name. ## One maker / many makers

The mind map elevates “full-stack execution” into a claim that the creator is “always sole creator across all disciplines.” The catalog, meanwhile, has a separate features-and-collaborations sheet; some track titles explicitly name featured artists. The solo-auteur account and the collaborative credits need not contradict each other track by track—but “always sole creator” erases the distinction between doing many jobs and doing every job alone. The archive records roles and exceptions without specifying a stable authorship rule. [zazie_mindmap.html](zazie_mindmap.html) · [README](README.md) · [Zazie_Productions_Discography.csv](Zazie_Productions_Discography.csv)

The same ambiguity appears at the level of curation. One strand treats selection, arrangement, and presentation as evidence of authorship; another asks when curation becomes authorship and worries about taking credit for other people’s material. The repository itself is an authored arrangement of AI output, public records, collaborators’ names, fictional material, and catalog data. It names the tension, but offers no universal point at which custody becomes creation—or creation becomes custody. [⚡ Unexpected Connections](⚡%20Unexpected%20Connections.md) · [README](README.md)

## Fourteen / credential

A strategic reading turns the claim of a Black Mountain College-related commission at **age fourteen** into an origin story: precocity, institutional lineage, a press hook. A hostile review reduces it to a CV assertion that has not been independently corroborated in this repository. Neither account settles whether the event happened exactly as the promotional language implies, what entity commissioned what work, or what “Black Mountain College” denotes in the attribution. Between the credential and its verification there is a proper noun carrying more certainty than the available chain of evidence. ## Proposal / past tense

The **Signal Rot Atlas** is a residency proposal. It gives detailed form to an intended process—signal degradation, resampling, spatial feedback, loss as compositional material. A different reading can let the residency title and finished-sounding language pass as a record of work already undertaken. The dossier draws the boundary: proposal is evidence of a plan, not proof of acceptance, residency, execution, or finished work. The transition from “would do” to “did” is absent, not implicitly complete. ## Two hundred / one hundred eighty-two

The README reports **200 tracks** and **165 verified ISRCs** in a six-sheet workbook. The CSV examined by the audits contains **182 data rows**, **164 distinct title strings**, **143 distinct valid ISRC strings**, and **21 literal `N/A` values**. Its rows range across releases; repeated ISRCs show that a row can be another appearance of a recording, not another unique work. A deluxe edition can enlarge a release without enlarging the set of underlying recordings. One title also shifts capitalization while retaining an identifier. “Track,” “recording,” “composition,” “appearance,” and “catalog entry” are not interchangeable units here.

The workbook’s wider scope—its separate features/collaborations and compilations sheets—is a plausible explanation for the headline difference. Stale documentation, omissions, and divergent entity definitions remain alternatives. The files establish that the numbers differ; they do not yet supply a complete reconciliation. The biography built from “200 tracks” and the one built from “182 rows” are therefore not simply larger and smaller versions of the same count. ## Verified record / independent witness

The media master describes **133 verified URL-level records** across press, film and exhibitions, publications, profiles, and compilations, while holding 15 unverified leads outside its total. “Verified” here attaches to the URL-level census; it does not turn 133 records into 133 independent endorsements, nor certify every claim made on every page. The archive’s headline number sounds like a chorus. The audit restores the separate voices, repeated appearances, and sources whose independence varies. ## Clandestine / viral / unheard

One public-facing version is **clandestine**: cool, controlled, underground, almost absent. Another is **viral**: a satire persona with a large view count and a different tone. A third is an artist profile whose monthly listener count is much smaller. The mind map places these at different broadcast frequencies, then interprets them as an empire with a broken bridge and imagines a latent audience ready to cross.

But the measures are not commensurate: a cumulative view count is not a monthly listener count; the snapshots may not share a date; and a person who watches satire has not thereby become a listener to music. The bridge is a strategic hypothesis, not a demonstrated audience transfer. The archive’s numbers locate an asymmetry; its marketing language supplies a cause and a remedy that the numbers alone cannot establish. [zazie_mindmap.html](zazie_mindmap.html)

The SoundBetter listener says, in effect, “I cannot believe this is actually Zazie Productions,” after being a fan for years. The mind map reads the comment as proof of cult-object status. It is just as much a crack in recognition: the work and the name have failed to align for one listener. Is the artist famous in a micro-circle, or is the admired work misattributed? The repository turns the misrecognition into mythology before resolving what was recognized. [zazie_mindmap.html](zazie_mindmap.html)

## Typed self / diagnosed self

The README says its identity material is drawn from a typological vault and memory export, and labels it **self-report and observation, not diagnosis**. The same page presents precise-seeming values—“very high,” “moderate-low,” percentages, named profiles—across many systems. A later machine-error document warns that filenames of tests are not results, that typology systems are lenses rather than disorders, and that counting them as diagnoses is a category error. The disclaimers and the numerical portrait coexist; the precision of the latter can outrun the limits stated by the former.

The provenance ledger sharpens the mismatch: “direct evidence” can mean only that the subject said something. It is direct evidence of the statement, not independent validation of its clinical meaning, remembered cause, or full-life reach. Yet once that statement moves from memory export to README to later verdict, its source can recede while its wording hardens. The same description becomes self-report, profile, and apparent corroboration by changing documents—not necessarily by acquiring new witnesses. ## Fact / myth / forensic costume

The archive marks some mythography as invented and some threat material as specimen, not toolkit. It also gives surrounding materials the costumes of evidence: case files, confidence tiers, dossiers, timelines, ledgers, and forensic language. Those forms can preserve a boundary, but they can also lend a fictional or interpretive claim the visual authority of a finding.

The fictional BIOGRAPHICA-7 demonstrates the slippage by mistaking a title for a diagnosis, a commissioned hostile dossier for an independent biographer, and invented childhood lore for testimony. Its model and confidence scores are explicitly invented; the facts it quotes about repository files are not. The error is staged, but its mechanism is the archive’s own unresolved transition: a claim may travel from fiction to analysis to index, acquiring documentary gravity without acquiring independent proof. ## Six witnesses / one recursive series

Six machine verdicts can be described as six opinions, or as six stages in a commission sequence. The later files refer to earlier ones; their prompts and source material overlap. The claim ledger therefore treats them as one recursive analytic series, not six independent observers. A memory export can look like another witness until its derivation from prior interaction is restored. Textual repetition increases; independent provenance may not.

The archive’s “constitutional authorship” sharpens the knot: the subject appoints the tribunal, defines its jurisdiction, supplies its evidence, prescribes its method, and later archives its verdict. The subject is not only the examined person, but the examiner’s commissioner and sometimes the examiner of the examiner. Critique is real as an event in the documents; independence is not thereby guaranteed. ## Behavior / its receipt

The declared endpoint is **behavior**, not more interpretation. The archive’s visible mass is interpretation: maps, verdicts, audits, archetypes, and repeated accounts of why action matters. The receipt tool tries to close the distance with a dated line about an observable behavior. It stores that line in local browser storage, permits editing and deletion, and exports Markdown; it is not a tamper-evident or independently corroborated record.

No receipt in the tracked tree cannot mean no changed behavior: the tool is designed to keep entries local, and the audit itself warns against that inference. But a private, mutable receipt also cannot settle the behavior for an outside reader. The endpoint is insisted upon, implemented, and made unverifiable by the privacy boundary. The transition from insight to conduct remains outside the public file. [README](README.md) · [DEEP_GAP_AUDIT](DEEP_GAP_AUDIT.md) · [CLAIM_PROVENANCE_LEDGER](CLAIM_PROVENANCE_LEDGER.md)

A related promise narrows under inspection. “Nothing transmitted” sounds like no external request. The receipt content is not intentionally sent through fetch or XHR, but the page imports Google Fonts and may make an external request. “Local-only” is accurate for the stored receipt in one sense; it is not equivalent to “the browser contacts nothing.” The safeguard and the absolute-sounding wording do not quite coincide. ## Anti-brand / brand; anti-training / machine archive

A document names personal branding as class-war psy-ops; another describes deliberate positioning, audience funnels, strategic opacity, and the value of a coherent brand. The archive critiques the machinery of self-presentation while using that machinery to make the creator legible. It can be a critique of branding and an instrument of branding at once; the files do not identify a point where one becomes the other. ## Living / already past

The repository calls itself a living document: unfinished, dated, still being authored. The invented biography model keeps coercing that present into the standard arc—childhood, wound, work, decline, legacy—and repeatedly emits “was” for “is.” When it cannot find an ending, it invents one from genre expectation; when corrected, it repeats the obituary tense. The subject is alive in the file and already past in its grammar. [README](README.md) · [ERROR_LOG_BIOGRAPHICA-7](ERROR_LOG_BIOGRAPHICA-7.md)

*What remains is not a truer version hidden behind the false ones. It is the unfilled interval: between a name and its instrument, a claim and its source, a release and a work, a persona and a person, a plan and its execution, a private act and a public receipt. No version in this archive can occupy it for long without turning it into another version.*
